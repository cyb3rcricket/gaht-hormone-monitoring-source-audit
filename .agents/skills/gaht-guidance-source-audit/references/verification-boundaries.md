# Verification boundaries

Read the current `PROTOCOL.md`, especially sections 19-21 and 29. It is authoritative.

## AI may

- locate candidate passages in an accessed authoritative source;
- draft extraction records and evidence notes when research-data changes are explicitly requested;
- suggest current controlled-vocabulary classifications;
- identify missing context, conflicts, and ambiguity;
- resolve bibliographic identity and correction metadata within the predefined source audit;
- inspect explicitly documented source dependencies;
- check synchronization and internal consistency across source, recommendation, and evidence-note records;
- run the existing validator and tests; and
- report discrepancies, provenance questions, and human-review requirements.

All AI-created research records begin as `pending`. AI identification of a likely value or classification is a draft, not verification.

## AI may not

- perform, simulate, or claim human verification;
- populate human-verifier identity or date on a human's behalf;
- establish clinical correctness, effectiveness, superiority, or consensus;
- invent missing source information, numerical bounds, context, or source relationships;
- resolve genuine ambiguity without documentary evidence;
- offer patient-specific interpretation or clinical advice;
- substitute PubMed, a secondary summary, or another publication for the authoritative included guidance source;
- treat literature discovery as an expansion of the predefined evidence base;
- convert documentary source checking into a literature review; or
- include a draft in findings, analysis, or visualization before protocol-defined verification and release conditions are met.

## Human decision boundary

The designated human verifier must compare the record directly with the authoritative source and explicitly approve it or return it for revision. Human verification covers documentary fidelity fields listed in Protocol section 19; it does not establish clinical correctness or patient applicability.
